# Software Engineering: Where From, Where To?

![A quiet workspace filled with system diagrams, notebooks, and glowing monitors late at night](assets/banners/where_to.png)

I started programming a long time ago, and looking back, it has been an incredible journey. There were plenty of ups and downs, but I now feel like I have reached one of the biggest milestones a college student can realistically aim for: an internship at Apple.

Working at Apple was always a dream of mine. To me, Apple never felt like a company that simply ships products at scale. It always stood out as a place that values elegant engineering, thoughtful design, and attention to detail.

As I wait for what comes next, I have found myself thinking constantly about growth again. The more I learn, the more I realize how many directions software engineering can branch into. Recently, I have been reflecting on what areas I truly want to master and what kind of engineer I eventually want to become.

## Infrastructure

Infrastructure engineering has become increasingly interesting to me. There is an enormous amount of depth in distributed systems: message queues, Kafka, RabbitMQ, leader election, sharding, load balancing, fault tolerance, and distributed coordination.

What fascinates me most is the idea of building systems that quietly power everything underneath. Most users never see them, but entire products depend on them functioning reliably.

At the same time, I wonder whether this is the direction I should prioritize right now, especially when AI dominates so much of the current engineering landscape.

## AI

AI is impossible to ignore today.

One thing I have realized is that there is a major difference between building applications around AI APIs and deeply understanding AI itself. Most of my experience so far has focused on integrating models into larger systems rather than training or researching models directly.

That realization has made me want to strengthen my fundamentals much more seriously. Instead of vaguely “learning AI,” I want to build a clearer roadmap around deep learning, model architectures, training pipelines, and the mathematics behind them.

More than anything, I want to move from simply using AI tools to understanding how and why they work.

## Games and 3D Graphics

Game development and graphics programming are areas I have always found exciting, even if they are not directly aligned with my main career goals.

There is something uniquely satisfying about graphics because it combines systems, math, rendering, simulation, and creativity all at once. It feels like one of the purest forms of engineering: turning code into an interactive world.

I have also started realizing that building games sounds far more fulfilling to me than simply playing them.

## Systems Design

Systems design is another area I want to improve significantly in.

Whenever I work on larger projects involving distributed systems, I often sketch out architectures and workflows before writing code. Doing this has helped me think more clearly about scalability, communication, and failure points.

At the same time, I know that most of my current understanding comes from intuition and experimentation rather than formal experience. There is a big difference between designing systems that “work” and designing systems the way experienced engineers at scale think about them.

That gap is something I want to close over the next few years.

## Hardware

The area that feels most interesting to me long-term is hardware.

I increasingly feel that the future of AI, especially robotics, will depend just as much on advances in hardware as on advances in software. Smarter models alone are not enough. Eventually, progress will depend on sensors, embedded systems, low-level optimization, power efficiency, and entirely new hardware architectures.

That realization has made me increasingly interested in understanding how hardware systems are designed at a deeper level. Software increasingly feels inseparable from the physical systems it runs on.

The idea of combining software, AI, and hardware into one unified field feels incredibly ambitious, but also deeply exciting.

Right now, I do not have a perfect answer for where all of this leads. But maybe that uncertainty is part of the process. The further I go into engineering, the less it feels like a straight path and the more it feels like exploring an enormous landscape one layer at a time. Perhaps it is best to do a Depth-First Search rather than a Breadth-First Search.
